Regional Food & Wine Tourism in Australia

Section 01

Why food and wine matter?

Start with the national 2024 evidence: travellers who included food markets, farm gates or wineries spent different amounts per night on their whole trips. The figures do not measure sales at those venues.

Farm-gate trips recorded the highest nightly spend of three food-and-wine activities

Farm-gate trips averaged A$367 per night, A$89 above the overall leisure/business benchmark. Winery and food-market trips averaged A$328 and A$294. These figures represent whole-trip spending, not attraction revenue.

The A$278 dashed benchmark is the published domestic overnight average. All values refer to spending across the entire trip, not food, wine or farm-gate purchases alone. A trip can include several activities, so the categories overlap.

Sources: Tourism Research Australia, 2024 Food Markets, Farm Gates and Wineries factsheets, p.1.

Section 02

Where are Australia's wine regions?

Australia’s wine regions give the story a geographic setting. Official geographical indications identify where wine regions are recognised, while the 2024 winery-trip survey describes tourism activity. They measure different things.

Wine tourism evidence: In 2024, domestic overnight trips that included winery visits averaged A$328 per night, compared with A$278 per night for the overall leisure/business-trip benchmark (Chart 01). These are whole-trip spending estimates, not winery purchases. The wine-region map below provides the geographic context for this activity.

Section 04

Overnight stays

Visitor numbers are only part of the story. Nights stayed show the scale of overnight tourism, while average length of stay shows how long trips last in selected regions. Both measures refer to all domestic overnight travel.

Section 06

What the evidence tells us

National 2024 data show that spending per night differs across trips including farm gates, wineries and food markets. These activities are associated with recognisable wine and food destinations, but regional visitor counts measure all-purpose tourism. Across 16 selected food-bowl-associated reporting regions, South Coast led both overnight trips and visitor-nights; the South Australian case study then shows why total expenditure, expenditure per night, and visitor-origin mix tell different stories. In FY2024–25, food-and-beverage products made up a greater share of Barossa than Hunter tourism consumption. These measures cover different populations and periods, and are not additive or equivalent to sales at food-and-wine attractions.

Original data providers and definitions

  • Tourism Research Australia: Agritourism in Australia and 2024 activity factsheets (food markets, farm gates and winery trips).
  • Wine Australia: Official geographical indication boundaries (wine-region geography, not visitation).
  • South Australian Tourism Commission: Visit to Regions, December 2024 (regional visits, nights and expenditure).
  • Tourism Research Australia: State and Regional Tourism Satellite Accounts (2024–25 consumption by product).

Reading the dashboard: Trip-including-activity statistics are different from all-purpose visitor counts for regions associated with food and wine. Region names and wine geographical indications do not necessarily share identical boundaries. Calendar-year 2024 and financial-year 2024–25 results are presented separately. Source survey estimates are subject to sampling variability and rounding.
